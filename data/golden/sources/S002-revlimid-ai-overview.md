The drug that recently sparked massive viral discussions regarding this extreme price disparity is Revlimid
(lenalidomide), a critical medication used to treat blood cancer. 
However, the reason for the price difference is slightly more nuanced than the FDA simply "not approving" it. The same chemical is widely available in India for a fraction of the cost due to differing patent laws and generic market competition, rather than a lack of FDA safety approval. 
The mechanics behind the massive price gap highlight how the two systems differ:
1. The Revlimid Price Disparity

    United States Price: A single branded Revlimid
    pill can cost between $900 and $1,000 out-of-pocket.
    India Price: Chemically identical generic versions manufactured in India sell for as low as $0.30 to $3.00 per pill. 

2. Why the Price Difference Exists
The vast price gap is primarily driven by legal and regulatory frameworks rather than chemical differences: 

    Patent Protections & Market Exclusivity: In the US, pharmaceutical companies are granted long periods of market exclusivity to recoup research and development (R&D) costs. During this time, they can set high prices because cheaper generic competitors are legally barred from entering the market. 

India's Robust Generic Industry: India is known as the "pharmacy of the world" because its legal system has historically favored the rapid production of generic drugs once basic patent criteria lapse. Multiple Indian manufacturers aggressively compete against one another, driving prices down to the cost of production. 
The Role of the FDA: The US FDA does approve generic lenalidomide, and many major Indian pharmaceutical manufacturing plants are fully FDA-approved. However, generic versions cannot always be freely sold or imported into the US until specific legal settlement windows or patent expirations clear the way. 

This pricing gap frequently leads American patients facing insurance denials to source their prescriptions internationally—such as via Canadian pharmacies that legally route orders directly to Indian manufacturers.
