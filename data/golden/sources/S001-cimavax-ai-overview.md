AI Overview
Some American patients with stage 4 lung cancer travel to Cuba to receive CIMAvax
, a therapeutic immunotherapy vaccine developed by Cuba's Center of Molecular Immunology. 
Why Americans Travel to Cuba for CIMAvax

    Lack of FDA Approval: CIMAvax
    is not approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), meaning American doctors cannot legally prescribe or administer it within the United States. 

Targeted Immunotherapy: Unlike traditional chemotherapy, CIMAvax targets the epidermal growth factor (EGF) protein that lung cancer cells need to grow, helping manage the disease as a chronic condition rather than destroying it via toxicity. 
U.S. Embargo & Restrictions: Long-standing U.S. trade and travel embargoes prohibit independent medical travel to Cuba and ban the commercial import of Cuban pharmaceuticals, forcing some patients to travel through third countries or transport refrigerated doses back independently. 
U.S. Clinical Trials: The Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center
in Buffalo, New York, has partnered with Cuban researchers to study CIMAvax through U.S. clinical trials, though full domestic approval remains an ongoing process.
